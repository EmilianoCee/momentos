Ideal op-amp and the virtual short

An op-amp multiplies the difference between its inputs by a huge number. Wire its output back to the − input and it chases its own tail: the output moves until the two inputs agree. Watch the difference shrink to microvolts as you raise A, and the gain settle on a ratio of two resistors.

The recipe

  1. V₊ = V₋: the output moves until the inputs agree.
  2. No current flows into either input.
  3. KCL at the pinned node gives v_out.

Right now

Loop gain

Ten times more A, ten times less error.

What should click

The output chases the inputs. v_out = A·(v₊ − v₋), so v₊ − v₋ = v_out/A. A few volts out of an amplifier with A = 10⁵ needs only tens of microvolts in. That is the virtual short: not a wire, a difference too small to matter.

Then the resistors decide. With v₋ pinned and no current into the input, KCL at the pinned node fixes v_out. A no longer appears: the gain is −R₂/R₁ or 1 + R₂/R₁.

The error is one over the loop gain. The gain falls short of ideal by 1/(1 + Aβ). Ten times more A, ten times less error.

Only with negative feedback. Swap the inputs and the output runs away from the balance instead of toward it, until a rail stops it. At a rail the output cannot move, and the inputs are no longer pinned.

Predict, then try

  • Inverting, R₁ = 1 kΩ, R₂ = 10 kΩ, v_in = 0.5 V. What is v_out, and what voltage sits on the − input?
    why−5 V, and almost exactly 0 V: the output moves until the − input matches the grounded + input. With A = 10⁵ it is 50 µV off.
  • Lower A from 10⁵ to 10³. Does the gain change by a factor of 100?
    whyNo. It goes from −9.9989 to −9.89, about 1% short. The error is 1/(1 + Aβ), and Aβ is still 91.
  • Swap the inputs. Where does the output go?
    whyTo a rail. Any difference now pushes the output the way that makes the difference bigger, so it runs until the rail stops it. The virtual short needs negative feedback.
  • Set v_in = 2 V with the rails on. Is the − input still at 0 V?
    whyNo. The output wants −20 V but stops at −12 V, so it can no longer pull the − input down: it sits at 0.73 V. The virtual short holds only while the output is free to move.
  • Follower with A = 1000. What is the gain?
    why1000/1001 = 0.999. All of the output is fed back, β = 1, so Aβ = 1000 and the error is 0.1%.

Where this shows up

  • feedback: the op-amp in a loop is G/(1 + GH) with G = A and H = β; when Aβ is large the gain is 1/β.
  • KCL: step 3 of the recipe is KCL at the pinned node, with no current into the input.
  • dividers: β is the divider R₁/(R₁ + R₂) from the output back to the − input.
  • non-ideal op-amp model: a finite A, input resistance and output resistance give errors of about 1/(1 + Aβ).
  • loop gain: Aβ is the loop gain, and it sets how close the circuit comes to ideal.
