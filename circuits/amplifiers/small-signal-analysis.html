Bias point and small-signal analysis

An amplifier holds a transistor at one point on its curve and adds a small wiggle. The wiggle sees only the slope at that point, g_m, so the curve can be replaced by its tangent and the transistor by a current source. Drag the point and the slope changes; make the wiggle big and the tangent stops telling the truth.

What should click

The signal sees a slope. For a small v_gs the curve is as good as its tangent, so i_d = g_m·v_gs. That one number, the slope at the bias point, is all the transistor contributes to the gain.

g_m belongs to the bias. g_m = k·V_OV = 2I_D/V_OV. More current or more overdrive, a steeper slope. The same transistor at another bias is a different amplifier.

Small means small next to V_OV. The second harmonic is v̂/(4V_OV) of the fundamental. With V_OV = 0.3 V, 30 mV is already 2.5 %.

DC sources hold still. In the small-signal circuit V_DD and V_GS are grounds: they do not change, so they add no signal. The bias decides g_m and r_o, and then leaves the picture.

Predict, then try

  • Move the bias from V_GS = 0.8 V to 1.1 V. Does g_m stay the same?
    whyNo. The slope of the curve grows with the overdrive: g_m = k·V_OV, so doubling V_OV from 0.3 V to 0.6 V doubles g_m. g_m belongs to the bias point, not to the transistor.
  • With the bias at 1.1 V, what happens to the gain?
    whyIt collapses. 270 µA through 10 kΩ pulls the drain down to 0.29 V, below V_OV = 0.6 V, so the transistor is in triode and is no longer a current source. Find the region before using the small-signal model.
  • Raise v̂ until the second harmonic reaches 10 %. How big is v̂ next to V_OV?
    whyAbout 0.4 V_OV: the rule is v̂/(4V_OV). "Small" is measured against the overdrive, not against V_DD.
  • Put v̂ = V_OV. Does the drain current rise and fall by the same amount?
    whyNo: it rises three times as far as it falls (270 µA up, 90 µA down at V_OV = 0.3 V). The curve bends up, and the tangent misses both.
  • In the small-signal circuit, where did V_DD go?
    whyTo ground. A DC source holds its voltage whatever the signal does, so it adds no signal: for the signal it is a short to ground.

Where this shows up

  • operating point: the bias is found first, with signals off, and it fixes every small-signal number.
  • transconductance: g_m is the slope at the bias point, 2I_D/V_OV for a MOSFET.
  • MOS small-signal model: the tangent redrawn as a circuit: g_m·v_gs with r_o across it and no gate current.
  • square law: the curve the bias point sits on.
  • MOSFET regions: the model holds only in saturation; the readout says when the bias leaves it.
  • common source: the stage in the second figure, with gain −g_m(r_o ∥ R_D).
