KCL and KVL on resistor circuits

Two rules solve every circuit on this page. Current that flows into a node flows out again. And voltage is a height: go round any loop and you arrive back at the height you left. Ohm's law, v = iR, ties the two together.

    circuit

    Slide a value and watch three things: how fast the dots move in each branch, the heights in the loop chart, and the two bars at the node staying the same length.

    What should click

    KCL is bookkeeping for charge. A node has nowhere to store charge, so at every instant the current arriving equals the current leaving. Watch the dots where a wire splits: the stream divides, and the number of dots per second going in equals the number coming out.

    KVL says voltage is a height. Every node sits at one height. A source lifts you up. A resistor lets you down by iR if you walk the way the current flows, and lifts you by iR if you walk against it. Go round any closed loop and you must arrive back at the height you left, so the rises and the drops cancel.

    Ohm's law is the exchange rate. KCL speaks about currents and KVL about voltages. v = iR converts one into the other, and that is what makes a circuit solvable: write KCL at each node whose voltage you do not know, replace every current by a difference of node voltages over R, and solve.

    Dividers are the two rules used once. One loop: resistors in series share a single current, so each drops voltage in proportion to its R. One pair of nodes: resistors in parallel share a single voltage, so each takes current in proportion to 1/R. The larger resistor gets the larger share of the voltage and the smaller share of the current.

    A negative answer is not a mistake. You choose the direction of each arrow before you know the answer. If the number comes out negative, the current flows the other way, and every equation you wrote is still correct.

    Predict, then try


    • Make R2 much larger than R1. Where does the voltage at A go?
      whyUp toward the source voltage. The same current flows through both, so the larger resistor takes nearly all of the drop.

    • Make R2 much larger than R1. Which resistor carries the current now?
      whyR1. Both see the same voltage, and the smaller resistance lets more current through.

    • Raise R4 a long way. What happens to the current in R2?
      whyIt goes up. Less current is drawn off through the right-hand branch, so node A rises and pushes more through R2.

    • Lower V2 until the dots in R3 stand still. What is V2 equal to at that moment?
      whyThe voltage at A. With the same height at both ends of R3 there is nothing to drive a current through it.

    Where this shows up

    • Nodal analysis is this method made systematic, and the equations it produces are a linear system, Gv = i.
    • Phasors and impedance: swap each R for a complex Z and everything on this page carries over to capacitors and inductors at one frequency.
    • Dividers set bias points, scale sensor outputs and load every source you will ever measure.
    • Work: voltage is work per unit charge, which is why KVL is really conservation of energy.
