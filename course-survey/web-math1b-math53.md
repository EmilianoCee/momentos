## Math 52 (formerly Math 1B): Calculus II
- Catalog: 4 units. Prerequisite: MATH 51 (formerly 1A). Credit restriction: no credit for MATH 1B/52 after N1B, H1B or XMATH 1B. The department page adds "Math 51, N1A, or equivalent". The description continues Math 51: integration techniques and applications, infinite sequences and series, and first- and second-order ODEs, including oscillation, damping and series solutions.

- Textbook: James Stewart, Single Variable Calculus (UC Berkeley custom paperback).
- Outline (department, approx. hours of 42): Ch. 7 techniques of integration, skip 7.6 (9); Ch. 8 sec 8.1 arc length etc. (1); Ch. 11 infinite sequences and series (12); Ch. 9 differential equations (8); Ch. 17 second-order differential equations (8); midterms and holidays (4).
- Topic candidates:
  1. integration by parts: the product rule run backwards, trading one integral for an easier one.
  2. trig integrals: powers of sin and cos reduce by identities to simple pieces.
  3. trig substitution: a square root of a^2 plus or minus x^2 becomes a right triangle.
  4. partial fractions: a rational function splits into simple poles you can integrate one by one. (EE)
  5. strategy for integrals: choose the method by the shape of the integrand.
  6. improper integrals: an area to infinity can still be finite, depending on how fast the tail falls. (EE)
  7. numerical integration: trapezoid and Simpson rules replace a curve by short straight or parabolic pieces.
  8. arc length: add up tiny hypotenuses along a curve.
  9. area and volume by slicing: integrate cross-section areas along an axis.
  10. average value and work: an integral weighted by a changing quantity.
  11. sequences and limits: a list of numbers either settles to a value or it does not.
  12. series and partial sums: an infinite sum means the limit of its running totals.
  13. geometric series: a ratio below 1 in size gives a total a/(1-r). (EE)
  14. divergence test: terms that do not shrink to zero cannot add to a finite number.
  15. integral test: a series behaves like the area under the matching curve.
  16. p-series: 1/n^p converges exactly when p > 1, the borderline for tails.
  17. comparison tests: a series smaller than a convergent one converges.
  18. alternating series: terms that flip sign and shrink trap the sum between partial sums.
  19. absolute and conditional convergence: some series converge only because of cancellation.
  20. ratio and root tests: compare the growth of terms to a geometric series.
  21. power series: a polynomial of infinite degree that works inside an interval. (EE)
  22. radius of convergence: the distance from the centre to the nearest trouble point. (EE)
  23. Taylor series: match a function's value and every derivative at a point. (EE)
  24. Taylor remainder: the error is bounded by the next term. (EE)
  25. standard series (e^x, sin, cos, 1/(1-x)): small-x approximations you can reuse. (EE)
  26. series as a calculator: differentiate or integrate a series term by term.
  27. direction fields: a first-order ODE draws a slope at every point and solutions follow them.
  28. separable equations: split x and y to opposite sides and integrate.
  29. exponential growth and decay: a rate proportional to the amount gives e^(kt), the RC discharge. (EE)
  30. linear first-order equations: an integrating factor makes the left side one derivative. (EE)
  31. Euler's method: step along the slope field in short straight steps.
  32. mixing and RC-type models: inflow minus outflow gives a first-order ODE with a time constant. (EE)
  33. equilibria and stability: solutions drift toward stable levels and away from unstable ones.
  34. second-order linear homogeneous ODE: two exponentials solve it, found from a quadratic in the rate. (EE)
  35. complex roots and oscillation: complex roots a +/- jb give a decaying sine, the RLC ringing. (EE)
  36. damping cases: under, critical and over damping depend on the sign of the discriminant. (EE)
  37. repeated roots: a double root adds a factor of t.
  38. nonhomogeneous equations: the response is the natural solution plus one particular solution. (EE)
  39. undetermined coefficients: guess the particular solution in the shape of the forcing.
  40. forced oscillation and resonance: driving near the natural frequency makes the amplitude grow. (EE)
  41. series solutions of ODEs: put a power series in the equation and solve for coefficients.
  42. Euler's formula link: e^(jt) = cos t + j sin t joins exponentials and oscillation. (EE)
- Changes worth knowing: renumbered from Math 1B to Math 52 effective Fall 2025 ("Prior to Fall 2025, this course was offered as Math 1B"); Math 1A became Math 51. Title unchanged (Calculus II). The catalog description now names oscillation and damping and series solutions of ODEs explicitly. Textbook unchanged per the department page. (The outline hours on the department page may predate renumbering; the page itself was updated for the new number.)

## Math 53: Multivariable Calculus
- Catalog: 4 units. Prerequisites: the department page says "1A-1B or equivalent" (not yet updated to 51-52 wording). The department "choosing courses" guidance, as reported by search only, says Math 51-52 with C- or better. I could not open the standard Math 53 catalog entry. The honors entry (MATH H53, https://undergraduate.catalog.berkeley.edu/courses/1147351) lists prerequisite "MATH 1B". Description: parametric equations and polar coordinates, vectors in 2D and 3D, partial derivatives, multiple integrals, and vector calculus up to the theorems of Green, Gauss and Stokes.

- Topic candidates:
  1. parametric curves: position as a function of time traces a path. (EE)
  2. polar coordinates: describe a point by distance and angle, natural for circles and spirals. (EE)
  3. area and arc length in polar and parametric form: the same slicing ideas with a new parameter.
  4. vectors and dot product: the dot product measures how much one vector points along another. (EE)
  5. cross product: it gives a vector perpendicular to two others, with length equal to the parallelogram area.
  6. lines and planes: a point plus a direction, or a point plus a normal.
  7. quadric surfaces: slice a surface with planes to see its shape.
  8. cylindrical and spherical coordinates: pick coordinates that match the symmetry. (EE)
  9. vector-valued functions: a curve in space with a tangent vector as velocity. (EE)
  10. velocity, acceleration and curvature: speed along the path versus how sharply it bends.
  11. functions of several variables: a surface over a plane, read with level curves.
  12. limits and continuity in several variables: the limit must agree along every path.
  13. partial derivatives: the slope when one input moves and the rest are held. (EE)
  14. tangent plane and linear approximation: zoom in and a surface looks flat. (EE)
  15. chain rule: rates multiply along each dependency path.
  16. directional derivative: the slope in any chosen direction is the gradient dotted with it. (EE)
  17. the gradient: it points uphill, steepest, and is perpendicular to level curves. (EE)
  18. maxima, minima and saddles: zero gradient, then curvature decides which. (EE)
  19. second derivative test: the Hessian sign pattern separates bowl, dome and saddle.
  20. Lagrange multipliers: at a constrained optimum the gradient lines up with the constraint's gradient. (EE)
  21. double integrals: volume under a surface by adding thin columns.
  22. iterated integrals and order swap: slice in x then y or the reverse for the same total.
  23. double integrals in polar form: the area element is r dr dtheta.
  24. triple integrals: mass or volume of a solid by adding tiny boxes.
  25. change of variables and the Jacobian: the Jacobian is the local area stretch factor.
  26. applications of multiple integrals: mass, centre of mass and moment of inertia.
  27. vector fields: an arrow attached to every point, like flow or force. (EE)
  28. line integrals of scalar fields: add a quantity along a curve weighted by length.
  29. line integrals of vector fields: work is force along the path. (EE)
  30. conservative fields and potentials: if work does not depend on path, a potential function exists. (EE)
  31. fundamental theorem for line integrals: work equals the potential difference between endpoints. (EE)
  32. Green's theorem: circulation around a loop equals the swirl summed inside. (EE)
  33. curl: a tiny paddle wheel's spin rate at a point. (EE)
  34. divergence: net outflow per unit volume at a point. (EE)
  35. parametric surfaces and surface area: a surface is a map from a flat patch, with the cross product as area element.
  36. surface integrals and flux: flux counts how much field passes through a surface. (EE)
  37. Stokes' theorem: circulation around a boundary equals the curl flux through the surface. (EE)
  38. divergence (Gauss) theorem: flux out of a closed surface equals divergence summed inside. (EE)
  39. examples of PDEs: heat and wave equations come from local balance laws. (EE)
