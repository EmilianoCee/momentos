- Topic candidates (the 7A list is drawn from the above plus the catalog line "mechanics and wave motion"; topics past week 11 are (from memory, not verified) as to exact order):
  1. units and estimation: every number carries a unit, and a rough guess tells you whether an answer is plausible.
  2. vectors: a quantity with size and direction adds tip to tail, and components make it arithmetic.
  3. position, velocity, acceleration: each is the slope of the one before it on a graph.
  4. constant acceleration: velocity grows in straight lines and position in parabolas.
  5. free fall: near Earth everything falls with about 9.8 m/s^2 whatever its mass.
  6. projectile motion: horizontal and vertical motion run independently and share only the clock.
  7. relative motion: velocities depend on who is watching, and they add as vectors.
  8. Newton's first law: with no net force, velocity does not change.
  9. Newton's second law (EE): the net force sets how fast momentum changes, F = ma.
  10. free-body diagram: draw only the forces on one object, then sum them.
  11. Newton's third law: forces come in pairs acting on two different objects.
  12. normal force and tension: surfaces and ropes push or pull exactly as hard as needed to hold constraints.
  13. friction: a contact force that opposes sliding, capped by a coefficient times the normal force.
  14. uniform circular motion: constant speed on a circle still means acceleration, pointing at the centre.
  15. centripetal force: it is a name for whatever net force points inward, not a new force.
  16. universal gravitation: attraction falls as one over distance squared.
  17. orbits: a satellite is a projectile that keeps missing the ground.
  18. work (EE): force times distance along the motion, so a force at right angles does none.
  19. kinetic energy and work-energy theorem (EE): net work changes speed squared.
  20. potential energy: stored work that a conservative force can give back.
  21. conservation of energy (EE): energy changes form, but the total of an isolated system does not.
  22. power (EE): the rate of energy transfer, in watts.
  23. energy diagrams: the slope of a potential energy curve is the force, and its minima are equilibria.
  24. linear momentum: mass times velocity, a vector that a force changes.
  25. impulse: force over time equals change in momentum.
  26. conservation of momentum: with no outside force, the total momentum stays fixed.
  27. collisions: momentum is always kept, kinetic energy only in elastic ones.
  28. centre of mass: the single point that moves as if all the mass and all outside forces sat there.
  29. rotational kinematics: angle, angular velocity and angular acceleration mirror position, velocity and acceleration.
  30. torque: force times lever arm, the twisting effect of a force.
  31. moment of inertia: how reluctant an object is to spin, depending on where its mass sits.
  32. rolling: a rolling wheel splits its energy between moving and spinning.
  33. angular momentum: spin that is conserved when no outside torque acts.
  34. static equilibrium: net force and net torque both zero, so nothing accelerates or turns.
  35. fluid pressure: pressure grows with depth by density times g times depth.
  36. buoyancy: a fluid pushes up with the weight of the fluid displaced.
  37. fluid flow and continuity: what flows in must flow out, so narrow pipes carry fast flow.
  38. Hooke's law and simple harmonic motion (EE): a restoring force proportional to displacement gives a sinusoid.
  39. oscillator energy (EE): energy swaps between kinetic and potential while the sum stays constant.
  40. pendulum: for small angles it is a harmonic oscillator with period set by length and g.
  41. damping and driving, resonance (EE): a drive near the natural frequency gives a large response.
  42. travelling waves (EE): a pulse carries a pattern and energy along while the medium stays put.
  43. wave speed, frequency and wavelength (EE): speed equals frequency times wavelength.
  44. superposition (EE): overlapping waves add at every point.
  45. standing waves and resonance in strings and pipes: reflection makes fixed patterns whose allowed frequencies are set by the length.
  46. sound and intensity: sound is a pressure wave, and its loudness is power per area.
  47. beats: two close frequencies swell and fade at the difference frequency.
  48. Doppler effect: motion of source or listener shifts the heard frequency.

- Topic candidates:
  1. temperature and thermal equilibrium: temperature is what is equal when two bodies stop exchanging heat.
  2. thermal expansion: heated solids stretch by a small fixed fraction per kelvin.
  3. ideal gas law: pressure, volume and temperature of a dilute gas are tied by PV = nRT.
  4. kinetic theory (EE): temperature is the average kinetic energy of molecules jostling in a box.
  5. equipartition: each way a molecule can move or store energy gets the same share, (1/2)kT.
  6. heat vs work: both are ways to move energy, and neither is stored in the body.
  7. specific heat and calorimetry: the temperature rise for a given heat is set by the heat capacity.
  8. phase changes: heat can go into breaking bonds with no temperature change.
  9. first law of thermodynamics: change in internal energy equals heat in minus work out.
  10. PV diagrams: the area enclosed by a cycle is the net work.
  11. heat conduction: heat flow is proportional to area and temperature difference over length.
  12. heat engines: an engine turns part of the heat into work and dumps the rest.
  13. entropy and second law: isolated systems drift toward the arrangement with the most ways to happen.
  14. electric charge (EE): charge comes in two signs, is conserved, and comes in units of e.
  15. Coulomb's law (EE): force between charges falls as one over r squared.
  16. electric field (EE): a field is the force per unit charge that a probe would feel at each point.
  17. field lines: lines start on positive charge, end on negative, and bunch where the field is strong.
  18. electric dipole: two close opposite charges give a field that falls faster than a single charge.
  19. electric flux: the count of field lines passing through a surface.
  20. Gauss's law (EE): net flux through a closed surface is the enclosed charge over epsilon-0.
  21. symmetry and Gauss: a symmetric charge makes the integral trivial for spheres, lines and sheets.
  22. conductors in electrostatics (EE): charge sits on the surface and the field inside is zero.
  23. electric potential energy: work stored in assembling charges, in joules.
  24. electric potential (EE): energy per unit charge, in volts, and field is its negative slope.
  25. equipotentials: surfaces of constant voltage always cross field lines at right angles.
  26. capacitance (EE): charge stored per volt, set by geometry.
  27. energy in a capacitor (EE): (1/2)CV^2 sits in the field between the plates.
  28. dielectrics: an insulator between plates cuts the field and raises capacitance.
  29. current and drift velocity (EE): current is charge per second, carried by slow-moving carriers.
  30. resistance and Ohm's law (EE): current proportional to voltage, with resistance set by material and shape.
  31. power in circuits (EE): P = IV, and in a resistor it all becomes heat.
  32. series and parallel networks (EE): series share current, parallel share voltage.
  33. Kirchhoff's laws (EE): charge is not lost at a node and energy is not gained around a loop.
  34. RC circuit (EE): charging follows an exponential with time constant RC.
  35. magnetic force on a moving charge (EE): force is at right angles to both velocity and field, so it turns the path without changing speed.
  36. cyclotron motion: a charge in a uniform field circles with radius mv over qB.
  37. force on a current-carrying wire (EE): a wire in a field feels the sum of the forces on its moving charges.
  38. magnetic dipole and torque: a current loop twists to line up with the field.
  39. Hall effect: moving charges pushed sideways build a voltage that shows their sign.
  40. Ampere's law (EE): the field circulating around a loop is set by the current through it.
  41. Biot-Savart law: each bit of current adds a small field that curls around it.
  42. magnetic flux and Faraday's law (EE): changing flux through a loop induces an EMF.
  43. Lenz's law (EE): the induced current opposes the change that caused it.
  44. motional EMF: a conductor moving across field lines gets a voltage along it.
  45. inductance (EE): a coil resists change in current and stores (1/2)LI^2.
  46. LR circuit (EE): current builds up with time constant L over R.
  47. LC oscillations (EE): energy sloshes between a capacitor and an inductor at 1 over root LC.

- Outline (Spring 2019): wk 1 Maxwell's equations, EM waves, energy flux (G 31); wk 2 ray optics, reflection, refraction (G 32); wk 3 thin lenses, ray tracing, lens combinations (G 33); wk 4 double-slit interference, diffraction, resolution (G 34-35); wk 5 polarization, reference frames and Einstein's postulates (G 35.11, S 1); wk 6 Lorentz transformations, time and length, midterm 1 (S 1); wk 7 paradoxes, Doppler effect, momentum and energy (S 1-2); wk 8 four-vectors, relativistic dynamics (S 2); wk 9 mass-energy conversion, blackbody radiation (S 3); wk 11 photoelectric effect, Compton scattering, the atomic nucleus, midterm 2 (S 3-4); wk 12 Bohr atom, matter waves (S 4-5); wk 13 1D Schrodinger equation, square well (S 5-6); wk 14 expectation values, operators, wave reflection and transmission (S 6-7); wk 15 general relativity, summary (S 2.5).
- Topic candidates:
  1. Maxwell's equations (EE): four laws tie fields to charges and currents, and to each other's change.
  2. displacement current (EE): a changing electric field acts like a current in Ampere's law.
  3. electromagnetic wave (EE): changing E makes B and changing B makes E, so the pair travels at c.
  4. wave speed from constants: c = 1 over root of mu-0 epsilon-0, so light is an electromagnetic wave.
  5. E, B and propagation direction (EE): the three are mutually at right angles, with E and B in step.
  6. energy flux and the Poynting vector (EE): the wave carries power per area along E cross B.
  7. radiation pressure: light carries momentum and pushes on what absorbs it.
  8. the spectrum: radio to gamma are the same wave at different wavelengths.
  9. reflection: the angle in equals the angle out.
  10. refraction and Snell's law: light bends at a boundary because its speed changes.
  11. total internal reflection: past a critical angle all light stays inside the denser medium.
  12. dispersion: refractive index depends on wavelength, so colours separate.
  13. thin lenses: a lens bends rays to a focus, with 1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i.
  14. ray tracing: three easy rays locate the image.
  15. real and virtual images: real images can be caught on a screen, virtual ones only seen.
  16. lens combinations: the image from one lens is the object for the next.
  17. mirrors: curved mirrors focus like lenses with the sign flipped.
  18. coherence and phase: waves only make steady patterns if their phase difference stays fixed.
  19. double-slit interference (EE): path difference of whole wavelengths adds, half wavelengths cancel.
  20. thin-film interference: reflections from front and back surfaces add or cancel depending on thickness.
  21. single-slit diffraction: a narrow opening spreads a wave, and narrower spreads more.
  22. diffraction grating: many slits make sharp, widely spaced bright lines.
  23. resolution: diffraction sets the smallest detail an aperture can separate.
  24. polarization: a transverse wave has a direction of E, and a polarizer keeps one component.
  25. Malus's law: transmitted intensity falls as cosine squared of the angle.
  26. Einstein's postulates: physics is the same in all inertial frames and c is the same for all.
  27. relativity of simultaneity: events that are simultaneous for one observer need not be for another.
  28. time dilation: a moving clock ticks slow by gamma.
  29. length contraction: a moving rod is shorter along its motion by gamma.
  30. Lorentz transformation: the formula that maps space and time between frames, keeping c fixed.
  31. relativistic Doppler effect: source motion shifts frequency, even for transverse motion.
  32. relativistic momentum and energy: E squared = (pc)^2 + (mc^2)^2.
  33. mass-energy equivalence: rest mass is a store of energy, E = mc^2.
  34. four-vectors and invariants: some combinations of space and time or energy and momentum are the same for everyone.
  35. blackbody radiation: a hot body glows with a spectrum set by temperature, and classical theory fails at short wavelengths.
  36. quantization of light: light comes in packets with energy hf.
  37. photoelectric effect (EE): electrons leave a metal only if each photon carries more than the work function.
  38. Compton scattering: a photon bounces off an electron and loses energy, showing it has momentum.
  39. atomic spectra and the Bohr atom: atoms emit only at set wavelengths because electron energies are discrete.
  40. matter waves: a particle has wavelength h over p, so electrons diffract.
  41. wave function and probability: the square of the wave function gives where you are likely to find the particle.
  42. uncertainty principle: sharper position means broader momentum, and the reverse.
  43. Schrodinger equation (EE): the wave function bends where its energy is below the potential, giving allowed states.
  44. particle in a box (EE): confinement forces discrete energies that grow as n squared over L squared.
  45. expectation values and operators: measurable quantities are averages computed from the wave function.
  46. tunnelling (EE): a wave decays inside a barrier but does not stop, so a particle can get through.
  47. energy bands (EE) (from memory, not verified as in the syllabus): many coupled wells spread levels into bands, which sets conductors vs insulators.
