MOS capacitor and threshold

Put a metal plate on thin glass over p-type silicon and raise its voltage. The silicon must answer with an equal and opposite charge, and it has three ways to do it: holes, bare acceptor ions, or a sheet of electrons. Drag the gate voltage and watch which answer it gives, and where the voltage goes.

The device

At the cursor

What should click

The body has three answers. Below V_FB, holes pile up at the surface. Above it, holes are pushed away and bare acceptors answer, deeper and deeper. Above V_T, electrons answer, right at the surface.

The voltage splits. V_GB − V_FB = V_ox + ψ_s: part across the oxide, part bending the body's surface. In depletion the body takes a large share; in inversion its share is pinned at 2|φ_p|.

Threshold is three steps. Cancel the built-in step (V_FB), bend the surface by 2|φ_p| so it is as n-type as the body is p-type, and put |Q_B,max|/C_ox across the oxide to hold the depletion charge.

Above V_T, a volt buys C_ox of electrons. Q_inv = −C_ox(V_GB − V_T). That sheet of electrons is the channel of a MOSFET, and its charge per volt is why C_ox appears in every MOSFET equation.

Predict, then try

  • Start at V_GB = 0 V and raise it slowly. Where does the extra gate charge find its partner in the body, below V_T and above it?
    whyBelow V_T, deeper in: the depletion region widens and uncovers more acceptors. Above V_T, at the surface: electrons gather there and the depletion stops at 144 nm.
  • With an n+ gate, is V_FB positive or negative?
    whyNegative, −0.951 V. The n+ gate sits 0.55 V above intrinsic and the p body 0.40 V below it, so the gate must be pulled down by their difference to flatten the bands.
  • Raise N_A tenfold. Does V_T go up or down, and which part grows most?
    whyUp, from 0.52 V to 2.18 V. 2|φ_p| grows by only 0.12 V; the depletion charge the oxide must hold grows about 3.4 times (√10, and a little more because 2|φ_p| grew too), and that term dominates.
  • Thin the oxide from 20 nm to 5 nm. What happens to V_T, and to the channel charge per volt above it?
    whyV_T falls to about 0.02 V, because a thinner oxide holds the same depletion charge with less voltage; and C_ox quadruples, so each volt above V_T buys four times the electrons.
  • In inversion, why does the slowly measured capacitance come back to C_ox while the fast one stays low?
    whySlowly, new electrons have time to appear right at the surface, one oxide-thickness from the gate. Fast, they do not, and the charge has to come from the edge of the depletion region, far below.

Where this shows up

  • MOS capacitor: the gate, oxide and body as a capacitor whose body plate can answer three ways.
  • accumulation, depletion, inversion: the three answers, in the first two figures.
  • threshold voltage: V_T = V_FB + 2|φ_p| + |Q_B,max|/C_ox, built term by term in the readout.
  • flatband voltage: V_FB = φ_p − φ_gate, where all charges vanish.
  • MOS C–V curve: C/C_ox against V_GB in the third figure.
  • triode region: above V_T the electron sheet C_ox(V_GB − V_T) is the channel that carries the drain current.
