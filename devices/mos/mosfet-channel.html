The MOSFET channel: triode, pinch-off and the square law

Above threshold the gate holds a sheet of electrons under the oxide, and the drain pulls them along. Every slice of that sheet must pass the same current, so where it is thin the electrons must move faster. Raise the drain voltage and watch the sheet thin toward the drain until it pinches off, and the current stop growing.

Region

At the probe

What should click

A sheet of charge, drifting. The gate holds C_ox(V_GS − V_T − V(x)) of electrons per area. The drain's field drags them along: I_D = W·Q(x)·μ·E(x), the drift current of a very thin layer.

Same current everywhere. Charge cannot pile up, so every slice carries the same I_D. Near the source the sheet is thick and a weak field will do; toward the drain it thins and the field must grow.

Pinch-off. When V_DS = V_OV the gate-to-channel voltage at the drain falls to V_T and the sheet there vanishes. More drain voltage drops across a short high-field gap; the rest of the channel, and so the current, hardly change.

Triode is a resistor, saturation is a current source. With small V_DS, I_D ≈ kV_OV·V_DS: a resistor set by the gate. Past pinch-off, I_D = (k/2)V_OV², set by the gate alone. That flat current is what amplifiers and current mirrors use.

Predict, then try

  • With V_DS = 0.2 V, is the voltage halfway along the channel 0.1 V?
    whyNo, 0.088 V. The sheet is thicker near the source, so the field there can be weaker and still carry the same current; more of the voltage drops near the drain.
  • Press "sweep V_DS". What happens to the channel at the drain end as V_DS passes V_OV = 0.5 V?
    whyIts thickness reaches zero: the gate-to-channel voltage there has fallen to V_T. Past that, the extra drain voltage drops across a short gap and the current stays at 250 µA.
  • Double V_OV from 0.5 V to 1 V with the transistor saturated. By how much does I_D grow?
    whyFour times. Twice the overdrive gives twice the charge per area and, at pinch-off, twice the voltage to drift through: (k/2)V_OV².
  • With a tiny V_DS, what is the transistor?
    whyA resistor, 1/(kV_OV) = 1 kΩ here, whose value the gate sets: more V_GS, more electrons, less resistance.
  • Move the probe toward the drain in saturation. What happens to the electrons' speed?
    whyIt climbs without limit in this model. Real electrons stop near 10⁷ cm/s, so the field the long-channel picture predicts near pinch-off is not what happens; the current is still set by the rest of the channel.

Where this shows up

  • triode region: the channel as a gate-controlled resistor that bends over as it thins toward the drain.
  • pinch-off: the drain end of the channel reaching zero charge at V_DS = V_OV.
  • square law: I_D = (1/2)μC_ox(W/L)V_OV² once the channel is pinched off.
  • MOSFET regions: cut-off, triode and saturation, split by V_GS = V_T and V_DS = V_OV.
  • drift current: each slice carries W·Q·μ·E, the same current from source to drain.
  • threshold voltage: the channel charge is C_ox(V_GS − V_T) at the source, from the MOS capacitor.
  • channel-length modulation: the gap at the drain grows with V_DS and shortens the channel, which tilts the flat curves.
