The diode: injection, diffusion and the exponential

Lower the junction's barrier and carriers spill over it, arriving on the far side as minority carriers. They can only diffuse away from the junction, and the slope of their crowd is the current. Move the voltage and watch the crowds, the slopes and the current grow together.

The diode

At the bias point

What should click

The barrier sets the edge. Lowering the barrier by V_D lets e^(V_D/(kT/q)) times as many carriers over it, so the minority density at each depletion edge rises by that factor.

The current is a slope. The neutral regions have no field, so the minority carriers can only diffuse. The current is qD times the slope at the edge: over W in a short region, over L = √(Dτ) in a long one.

The light side does the work. Each side's minority density starts at n_i²/N, so the lightly doped side has more to inject and carries most of the current.

The tangent is a resistance, the crowd is a capacitance. Near the bias point a small wiggle sees the slope I_D/(kT/q), so r_d = 26 Ω at 1 mA. The carriers waiting in the neutral regions grow with the current, so the diode stores charge like a capacitor C_d that grows with the current too.

Predict, then try

  • Raise V_D from 0.65 V by 60 mV. By what factor does the current grow, and what happens to the profiles' shape?
    whyAbout ten times, because e^(0.0599/0.026) = 10. The edge densities rise tenfold and the profiles scale with them; their shape does not change, so neither does anything except the size.
  • The p side is doped ten times more heavily than the n side. Which carrier carries most of the current?
    whyHoles injected into the n side, 93 %. The minority density on each side is n_i²/N, so the lightly doped side starts with ten times more minority carriers and receives ten times more.
  • With W = 1 µm, lower τ from 1 µs to 1 ns. Does the current change much? Now set W = 100 µm and do the same.
    whyAt 1 µm, by only about a third, and only near the end: L is many times W until τ nears 1 ns, so the profile stays a straight line and W sets the slope. At 100 µm the diode is long and the carriers die within L, so a thousandfold shorter lifetime makes L √1000 times shorter, the slope that much steeper, and the current 32 times larger.
  • Reverse the bias to −0.3 V. Why does the current not grow with more reverse voltage?
    whyThe edges are already emptied to almost zero minority carriers. The current is the few that diffuse to the edge and are swept across, and emptying an empty edge further changes nothing: I_D ≈ −I_S.
  • At 0.65 V the diode carries 107 µA. What is its small-signal resistance, and what happens to it at ten times the current?
    whyr_d = 26 mV/107 µA = 243 Ω. At ten times the current the exponential is ten times steeper, so r_d falls to a tenth, 24.2 Ω.

Where this shows up

  • pn junction in equilibrium: forward bias tips the equilibrium balance, and the diffusion that was cancelled is now the current.
  • diffusion current: the current in each neutral region is pure diffusion, qD times the slope of the profile.
  • mass-action law: the minority densities n_i²/N_A and n_i²/N_D set the size of I_S.
  • small-signal analysis: r_d = (kT/q)/I_D is the first small-signal model: the tangent of a curve at its bias point.
  • BJT action: a forward-biased emitter junction injects minority carriers exactly this way, and the collector catches them.
