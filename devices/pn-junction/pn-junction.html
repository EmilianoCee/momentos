The pn junction: charge, field and potential

Where p-type meets n-type silicon, carriers spread across and leave bare ions behind. Those ions make a field, and the field makes a step in potential that stops the spreading. Change the dopings and the bias and watch the charge, the field and the potential, each built from the one above it.

The step

The depletion region

What should click

Equal charge, unequal depth. The ions on the two sides must cancel, N_A x_p = N_D x_n, so the region reaches deep into the lightly doped side and barely into the heavy one.

Charge, then field, then potential. Gauss's law turns the boxes into a triangle of field that peaks at the junction, and integrating again gives a step of φ_bi − V_D, mostly on the light side.

Reverse bias widens it as a square root. A bigger step needs more charge, and the charge grows as the depth, but the step grows as the depth squared, so W ∝ √(φ_bi − V_D). The charge that moves sits at the edges, so the junction is a capacitor ε_s/W that shrinks as the bias grows.

Equilibrium is a tug of war. Holes diffuse toward the n side and the field drives them back, each at about 10⁵ A/cm². They cancel only because D/μ = kT/q, and that is the Einstein relation.

Predict, then try

  • N_A is ten times N_D. Before looking, where does most of the depletion region lie?
    whyOn the n side, the lightly doped one: x_n = 103 nm against x_p = 10.3 nm. The two boxes must hold equal charge, so the side with a tenth of the ions per cm³ needs ten times the depth.
  • Where is the field strongest, and why there?
    whyAt the junction, x = 0. Coming in from the p side the field grows with every negative ion it passes, and from there on it shrinks with every positive ion, so it peaks where the two kinds of charge meet.
  • Apply 3 V of reverse bias. By how much does W grow, and what happens to C_j?
    whyThe step grows from 0.898 V to 3.898 V, 4.34 times, so W grows √4.34 = 2.08 times, to 235 nm, and C_j = ε_s/W falls by the same factor, to 44.0 nF/cm².
  • Raise N_D tenfold, to 10¹⁸ cm⁻³. How much does φ_bi rise?
    whyBy 59.9 mV, one decade: φ_bi is the n side's potential minus the p side's, and the n side's moved up one step on the doping ladder.
  • In the lower figure the hole diffusion current is 5×10⁵ A/cm², yet nothing flows. Now set D_p/μ_p to 30 mV. What would happen, and what does that tell you?
    whyA net hole current of about 10⁵ A/cm² would flow in a junction with nothing connected, which is impossible in equilibrium. So D/μ must be exactly kT/q: the Einstein relation.

Where this shows up

  • doping: the dopings set both boxes of charge and both ends of the potential step.
  • potential and doping: φ_bi is the n side's potential from intrinsic minus the p side's.
  • MOS capacitor: a MOS capacitor's body depletes exactly like the light side of a one-sided junction.
  • body effect: a MOSFET's body is a reverse-biased junction under its channel, and V_SB widens it.
  • MOSFET capacitances: the drain and source of a MOSFET are pn junctions with a C_j of their own.
  • forward bias: lowering the step lets diffusion win, which is where a diode's current comes from.
