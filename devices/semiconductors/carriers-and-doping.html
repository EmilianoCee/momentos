Carriers and doping

Pure silicon has a few free electrons, each with a hole left behind. Add donors and the electrons outnumber the holes by a million; add acceptors and the reverse. Move the doping and the temperature, and watch the electrons, the holes and the Fermi level move together.

What should click

n and p are mirror images. Pairs are made at a rate set by temperature and destroyed at a rate set by n·p, so in equilibrium np = n_i². On a log scale n sits as far above n_i as p sits below it.

The dopants cancel. Each donor's spare electron can fill one acceptor's missing bond, so only N_D − N_A counts.

The log scale is an energy scale. n = n_i·e^((E_F − E_i)/kT), so each tenfold step in density moves E_F by (kT/q)·ln 10 = 59.9 meV. The same number in volts is the material's potential from intrinsic silicon, and every built-in potential is a difference of two of them.

Heat makes pairs. n_i is a Boltzmann factor in E_g/2kT and doubles about every 9 K near 300 K. A doped sample's majority density stays at the doping until n_i catches up; then it is no longer n-type or p-type.

Predict, then try

  • Start from pure silicon (both sliders at none), then add 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ donors. The electrons rise six decades. What do the holes do?
    whyThey fall six decades, to 10⁴ cm⁻³. Pairs are still made at the same rate, but with a million times more electrons about, each hole is filled a million times sooner, so np stays at n_i².
  • Raise N_D from 10¹⁶ to 10¹⁷ cm⁻³. How far does E_F move?
    whyUp by 59.9 meV, one decade's worth. E_F follows the logarithm of the density, so every tenfold step moves it the same amount.
  • Set N_D to 4×10¹⁶ cm⁻³ (slider at 16.6) and N_A to 10¹⁷ cm⁻³. n-type or p-type, and how many majority carriers?
    whyp-type, with about 6×10¹⁶ cm⁻³ holes. Each donor's spare electron fills one acceptor's missing bond, so the two cancel and only the difference is left.
  • Keep N_D = 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ and warm the sample from 300 K toward 700 K. When does it stop being n-type?
    whyThe label changes at 590 K, where n_i has reached a tenth of the doping and n is no longer 100 times p. Until then n stays pinned at N_D while p climbs as n_i²/N_D; near 700 K heat makes pairs about as fast as the donors gave electrons, and n and p close in.
  • Push N_D to 10¹⁹ cm⁻³. What does the warning mean?
    whyE_F has come within 3kT of the conduction band. The logarithm assumes most states near E_F are empty, which is no longer true, so it places E_F too low; the silicon is close to degenerate.

Where this shows up

  • drift current: n and p set the current a field drives, J = q(nμ_n + pμ_p)E, and the minority term is decades down.
  • built-in potential: the step across a junction is φ_n − φ_p, the two potentials read off this ladder.
  • threshold voltage: the MOS body's φ_p = −(kT/q)·ln(N_A/n_i) is this tool's E_F for p-type material.
  • diode I–V: the minority densities n_i²/N_A and n_i²/N_D set a diode's saturation current.
