Drift and mobility

Inside a silicon bar the carriers race about at ten million centimetres per second, bouncing off the lattice and the dopant ions. A field cannot stop that; it only tilts it. Between collisions each carrier gains a little speed along the field, and on average they creep along at v = μE. That creep is the current.

What should click

Fast motion, slow drift. The carriers move at about 2×10⁷ cm/s in random directions. A field of 1 kV/cm adds an average of about 10⁶ cm/s along it: a small tilt on a fast random walk.

Mobility is a push that survives. Between collisions the field accelerates a carrier by qE/m*. A collision every τ_c undoes it, so the average drift is (qτ_c/m*)E = μE. Heavier carriers or more frequent collisions, lower μ.

Dopants are obstacles too. Every ionised dopant scatters carriers. Tenfold doping gives tenfold carriers but a lower μ, so ρ falls less than tenfold.

Current follows the field. Electrons drift against E, holes along it; both currents point along E: J = q(nμ_n + pμ_p)E. In a doped bar the majority term is all that matters.

Predict, then try

  • At 1 kV/cm, how fast do the electrons drift compared with how fast they move?
    whyAbout a twentieth: 1.2×10⁶ cm/s of drift on top of 2.3×10⁷ cm/s of random motion. Watch carrier 0: it zigzags in every direction, and only its average creeps against the field.
  • Raise the doping from 10¹⁶ to 10¹⁷ cm⁻³. Does the resistivity fall ten times?
    whyNo, about 6.4 times. Ten times more carriers, but the extra ions scatter them and μ_n falls from 1218 to 777 cm²/(V·s).
  • Switch to p-type at the same doping. Is the bar a better or worse conductor?
    whyWorse, about 2.8 times: holes are heavier and their mobility is lower.
  • Which way do the electrons go, and which way does their current flow?
    whyThe electrons drift against E; their charge is negative, so their current flows along E. Holes drift and carry current along E. Both currents point the same way.
  • Turn the field up to 100 kV/cm. Does the drift keep growing ten times per decade?
    whyNo: figure 3 bends over near 10⁷ cm/s, close to the thermal speed. The carriers lose energy to the lattice as fast as the field gives it. The simulation, which keeps τ_c fixed, does not show this.
  • In an n-type bar with 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ donors, why can the holes be ignored?
    whyThere are 10⁴ cm⁻³ of them against 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ electrons: twelve decades fewer, and slower too.

Where this shows up

  • drift current: J = q(nμ_n + pμ_p)E, the field's small push averaged over many collisions.
  • mobility: μ = qτ_c/m*; it falls with doping and is 2 to 3 times higher for electrons.
  • resistivity: ρ ≈ 1/(qNμ), set by the majority carriers alone.
  • velocity saturation: the drift stops near 10⁷ cm/s, close to the thermal speed.
  • doping: the dopants set the carriers and, as obstacles, the mobility.
  • triode region: a MOSFET's channel is a sheet of electrons drifting in the field from source to drain: the same J = qnμE.
