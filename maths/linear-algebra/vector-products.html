Vector products

There are two ways to multiply two arrows, and both come from one picture. Split b into the part that lies along a and the part that lies across it. The dot product measures the first part. The cross product measures the second.

What should click

One triangle, two products. Drop a perpendicular from the tip of b onto the line through a. One leg of the triangle runs along a: it is the shadow b casts on a. The other leg runs across a: it is how far the tip of b stands off from that line. Multiply the along leg by |a| and you have a·b. Multiply the across leg by |a| and you have a×b, which is also the area of the parallelogram: base times height.

Cosine and sine. Turn b through a full circle. The along leg follows cos θ and the across leg follows sin θ, so each product is at its largest exactly when the other is zero. Between them they always account for everything: (a·b)² + (a×b)² = |a|²|b|².

What the signs mean. a·b turns negative when b leans against a, so that its shadow falls behind the origin. a×b turns negative when b is clockwise from a: the parallelogram has flipped over.

In three dimensions the cross product is an arrow. It stands at right angles to the plane of a and b, its length is the area of the parallelogram, and the right-hand rule gives its direction: curl your fingers from a round to b and your thumb points along a×b. The flip of the parallelogram is that arrow going from out of the page (⊙) to into it (⊗).

Predict, then try

  • Slide the tip of b parallel to a. Which product should not change?
    whyThe cross product. The across leg stays the same length, so the parallelogram keeps its height.
  • Now slide it straight toward the line of a. Which one holds still this time?
    whyThe dot product. The along leg does not change.
  • Make a twice as long. What happens to the shadow, and to a·b?
    whyThe shadow of b on a depends only on the direction of a, so it stays put. The product doubles.
  • At what angle are the two products equal?
    whyWhere cos θ = sin θ: at 45°, and again at −135° where both are negative.

Where this shows up

  • Fourier series: each coefficient is this shadow, with the signal as b and one harmonic as a.
  • AC power: with the voltage and current phasors as the two arrows, half of a·b is the average power and half of a×b is the reactive power.
  • Complex numbers: multiply the conjugate of a by b and you get (a·b) + j(a×b), both products in one number.
  • Determinant: in the plane, a×b is det[a b].
  • Work is F·d, and the magnetic force is qv×B.
