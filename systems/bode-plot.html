Bode plot from poles and zeros

Each pole bends the magnitude down by 20 dB per decade from its corner on, and each zero bends it up. Draw the straight lines, add them, and the true curve follows them except near the corners. Move the cursor and watch a sine wave go in and come out smaller and later.

What should click

The corner is where real and imaginary are equal. At ω = p the factor 1 + jω/p is 1 + j: its length is √2 and its angle 45°. So one pole leaves 1/√2 of the amplitude (−3 dB, half the power) and lags by 45°, never half the amplitude and never 90°.

On log axes, factors add. 20 log₁₀ of a product is a sum, and the angle of a product is a sum of angles. Each pole is one straight line, bending down 20 dB per decade from its corner; each zero bends up. Draw them one at a time and add.

The phase is slow. A pole's angle goes from 0° to −90° as ω goes from far below to far above the corner, but a decade below it is already −5.7° and a decade above it still has 5.7° to go. That is why the sketch spreads the turn over two decades.

rad/s is not Hz. 1/(RC) is an angular frequency. The corner in Hz is 1/(2πRC), six times smaller.

Predict, then try

  • RC low-pass, R = 1 kΩ, C = 1 µF. Put the cursor at the corner. Is the output half the input?
    whyNo. It is 1/√2 = 0.707 of the amplitude, −3 dB. Half the power, not half the voltage.
  • At the same corner, what is the phase: −90° or −45°?
    why−45°. At the corner 1 + jω/ω_c = 1 + j, whose angle is 45°. The phase reaches −90° only far above the corner.
  • The corner is at 1000 rad/s. What frequency in Hz is that, and where does a 1000 Hz sine land on the plot?
    why1000 rad/s is 159 Hz. A 1000 Hz sine is ω = 6283 rad/s, almost a decade above the corner, at about −16 dB.
  • Double C. Which way does the corner move, and by how much on the log axis?
    whyTo half the frequency, ω_c = 1/(RC). Halving is 0.301 of a decade to the left, and the whole curve slides with it without changing shape.
  • Free mode, two poles at 10² and 10⁴ rad/s. What is the slope at 10³ rad/s and at 10⁶ rad/s?
    why−20 dB per decade between the two corners, −40 dB per decade above both. Each pole adds its own −20.

Where this shows up

  • transfer function: H(s) read off the circuit is the thing being drawn; each factor is one line on the plot.
  • poles and zeros: each pole bends the magnitude down and each zero bends it up, at the pole's distance from the origin.
  • first-order systems: an RC circuit is one pole; its corner 1/τ is where the gain starts to fall.
  • frequency response: the Bode plot is H(jω) drawn on log axes so that products become sums.
  • phasors & impedance: the divider with Z = 1/(jωC) is where H(jω) comes from.
  • loop gain: the phase margin is read off the Bode plot of the loop gain.
