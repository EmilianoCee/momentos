RC and RL step response

A capacitor's voltage and an inductor's current cannot jump. So when a switch moves, they start where they were and slide exponentially to where the circuit will leave them. Three numbers solve it: the start, the final value and τ.

What should click

The state cannot jump. A capacitor's voltage is its charge over C, and charge takes time to move. An inductor's current is its flux over L. So v_C and i_L at t = 0⁺ are what they were at t = 0⁻. Everything else, including i_C and v_L, can jump.

Two resistor circuits give the two ends. At t = 0⁺ the capacitor is a voltage source and the inductor a current source, holding their values. At t → ∞ the capacitor is open and the inductor a short. Solve each and you have the jump and the final value.

τ is the resistance the store sees. Turn the source off and look out from the store's terminals: R_th. Then τ = R_th·C or L/R_th. A resistor across the store counts.

63% of what is left, every τ. After τ the change is 63% done, after 3τ 95%, after 5τ 99%. The starting slope, kept up, would finish at τ: the tangent.

Predict, then try

  • Double C. Does the capacitor reach 5 V sooner or later?
    whyLater. τ = RC doubles: twice the charge has to move through the same resistor at the same current.
  • Close the switch with the capacitor at 0 V. What is the current the instant after, and what is it a long time later?
    why5 mA, then 0. At t = 0⁺ the capacitor is a 0 V source, so all of V_S sits across R₁. At t → ∞ it is an open circuit.
  • Add R₂ = 1 kΩ across the capacitor. What happens to τ and to the final voltage?
    whyBoth halve. The capacitor sees R₁ ∥ R₂ = 500 Ω, and the divider leaves it 2.5 V.
  • After one τ, is the capacitor at 5 V?
    whyNo: at 63% of the way, 3.16 V. It is within 1% of 5 V only after about 5τ. The tangent line is what reaches 5 V at τ.
  • Switch to the inductor and add R₂. Does the inductor get faster or slower?
    whyFaster. τ = L/R_th, and R₂ makes R_th smaller. A capacitor and an inductor answer a smaller resistance in opposite ways.
  • Square wave with the period at 2τ. Does the capacitor ever reach 0 V or 5 V?
    whyNo. Each half period is one τ, so it goes 63% of the way and turns back. It settles into a ripple between about 1.34 V and 3.66 V.

Where this shows up

  • first-order systems: one store, one τ: the step response covers 63% of what is left every τ.
  • step response: the switch on is a step input; where the curve settles is the circuit's gain for a constant input.
  • Thévenin and Norton: the store sees a Thévenin source; its resistance is τ's R and its voltage is the final value.
  • KVL: at t = 0⁺ the store is a source and KVL around the loop gives the jump.
  • Bode plot: the same circuit in the frequency domain is one pole at 1/τ.
